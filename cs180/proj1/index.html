Approach

Each digitized plate contains three grayscale exposures in vertical order: blue, green, and red. I split the plate into three equal-height layers, hold blue fixed, and independently translate green and red until each best matches blue. Reported offsets use (dx, dy), where positive dx moves right and positive dy moves down.

Matching metric

Every candidate is scored with normalized cross-correlation (NCC). Before scoring, I exclude 10% from each plate edge, or a larger margin when required by the search displacement. This keeps the original plate borders and shifted rollover pixels from dominating the comparison.

Single-scale search

For the three small JPEGs, I exhaustively test every horizontal and vertical displacement from −15 through 15 pixels and select the shift with the largest NCC score.

Image-pyramid search

Large plates are repeatedly halved with area interpolation. I search ±15 pixels at the coarsest level, double that displacement at each finer level, and refine it within ±2 pixels. The selected full-resolution shifts are applied before stacking the channels in OpenCV BGR order.

Why Emir fails

The red layer is visibly misplaced. Emir’s clothing has very different intensities in the red and blue exposures, so raw-pixel NCC treats an incorrect location as the strongest match. A feature-based comparison, such as image edges, would be less sensitive to this cross-channel brightness difference.
